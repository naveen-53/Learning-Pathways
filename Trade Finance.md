# Trade Finance & Foreign Trade Operations — Complete Learning Guide

> **Purpose:** A practical learning guide for understanding the major Trade Finance and Foreign Exchange operations used by banks and corporate customers.
>
> **Topics covered:** Inward Remittance, Outward Remittance, Import Letter of Credit, Import Collection, Export Letter of Credit, Export Collection, Shipping Guarantee, Bank Guarantee, Finance Request, Import Regularization, and Export Regularization.
>
> **Important:** Exact forms, regulatory rules, document requirements, timelines, limits, charges, and accounting treatment vary by country, bank, product, and transaction type. The examples below are simplified learning scenarios rather than transaction-processing instructions.

---

# 1. First Understand the Big Picture

## What is Trade?

In banking, **Trade** generally refers to financial services supporting the buying and selling of goods and services, especially when the buyer and seller are in different countries.

A typical international trade transaction has:

```text
Exporter / Seller
       |
       | Goods / Services
       v
Importer / Buyer
       |
       | Payment
       v
Exporter
```

Banks become involved because international transactions create risks:

- Will the exporter actually receive the money?
- Will the importer receive the goods?
- Will the exporter ship the correct goods?
- Are the shipping documents genuine and compliant?
- What happens if the buyer cannot pay?
- What happens if documents arrive before the goods?
- Does the transaction comply with foreign-exchange regulations?

Trade products such as **Letter of Credit, Collection, Guarantee, Remittance, and Trade Finance** help manage these risks.

# 2. Important Parties in Trade

Before learning the products, understand these parties.

| Party | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Importer | Buyer of goods/services from another country |
| Exporter | Seller of goods/services to another country |
| Applicant | Party requesting a bank to issue a facility/instrument; e.g. importer for an LC |
| Beneficiary | Party receiving payment or benefiting from an instrument |
| Issuing Bank | Bank that issues an LC/guarantee |
| Advising Bank | Bank that advises an LC to the beneficiary |
| Confirming Bank | Bank that adds its own payment undertaking to an LC |
| Nominated Bank | Bank authorized under an LC to pay/accept/negotiate documents |
| Remitting Bank | Bank sending documents/payment instructions |
| Collecting Bank | Bank receiving documents/payment from another bank for collection |
| Presenting Bank | Bank presenting collection documents to the drawee/buyer |
| Carrier | Transport company carrying the goods |
| Customs | Government authority controlling import/export clearance |
| Insurance Company | Provides cargo/transport insurance where applicable |

# 3. Inward Remittance

## Definition

**Inward remittance** is money received by a customer or beneficiary from another person, company, bank, or institution located outside the country.

Simple meaning:

> **Money coming into the country from abroad = Inward Remittance.**

## Basic Flow

```text
Foreign Sender
      |
      | Payment
      v
Foreign Bank
      |
      | International payment message
      v
Beneficiary Bank
      |
      v
Beneficiary / Exporter / Customer
```

## Example

An Indian software company provides software services to a US company.

The US company needs to pay USD 10,000.

```text
US Company
    |
    | USD 10,000
    v
US Bank
    |
    v
Indian Bank
    |
    v
Indian Software Company
```

The USD 10,000 received by the Indian company is an **inward remittance**.

## Common Types / Sources

### 1. Export proceeds

Money received from an overseas buyer for exported goods.

### 2. Service export proceeds

Money received for software development, consulting, IT services, etc.

### 3. Salary/remuneration

Money sent from an overseas employer or source.

### 4. Family/personal remittance

Money sent by an individual to a family member.

### 5. Investment-related receipt

Money received for eligible investment purposes.

### 6. Refund / other legitimate receipts

Money received from an overseas party for a valid commercial or personal reason.

## Important Checks

A bank may need to determine:

- Who sent the money?
- Who is the beneficiary?
- Purpose of payment
- Currency
- Amount
- Supporting documents where required
- Sanctions/AML screening
- Foreign-exchange regulatory requirements
- Whether the transaction relates to an export/import/service

## Scenario

### Scenario: Exporter receives payment

ABC India exports machinery worth USD 50,000.

The foreign buyer sends USD 50,000.

**Transaction type:** Inward remittance.

**Business meaning:** Exporter is receiving its trade proceeds.

# 4. Outward Remittance

## Definition

**Outward remittance** is money sent from a customer or bank in one country to a beneficiary outside that country.

Simple meaning:

> **Money going from the country to another country = Outward Remittance.**

## Basic Flow

```text
Indian Customer
      |
      | INR / foreign currency funding
      v
Indian Bank
      |
      | International payment
      v
Foreign Bank
      |
      v
Foreign Beneficiary
```

## Example

An Indian company imports machinery from Germany for EUR 30,000.

The Indian company needs to pay the German supplier.

```text
Indian Importer
      |
      v
Indian Bank
      |
      | EUR 30,000
      v
German Bank
      |
      v
German Exporter
```

This payment is an **outward remittance**.

## Common Types

### 1. Import payment

Payment for imported goods.

### 2. Service payment

Payment to an overseas service provider.

### 3. Education-related payment

Eligible payment for education expenses.

### 4. Travel-related payment

Eligible foreign-exchange payment for travel.

### 5. Investment-related payment

Eligible overseas investment/remittance.

### 6. Other legitimate commercial payments

Payments supported by the relevant purpose and documentation.

## Important Checks

Banks may check:

- Beneficiary details
- Purpose
- Currency
- Amount
- Supporting documents
- Regulatory requirements
- AML/sanctions screening
- Customer account/funding
- Applicable taxes/charges where relevant

---

# 5. Inward vs Outward Remittance

| Feature | Inward Remittance | Outward Remittance |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Money comes in | Money goes out |
| Typical customer | Exporter/recipient | Importer/payer |
| Example | Foreign buyer pays Indian exporter | Indian importer pays foreign supplier |
| Bank role | Receive and credit | Process and send |
| Currency movement | Foreign country → local country | Local country → foreign country |

### Easy Memory Trick

```text
INWARD  = IN
OUTWARD = OUT
```

# 6. Import Letter of Credit (Import LC)

## Definition

An **Import Letter of Credit (LC)** is a bank-issued undertaking made at the request of an importer to pay a beneficiary, usually an overseas exporter, provided the beneficiary presents documents that comply with the LC terms.

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/naveen-53/Learning-Pathways/main/Images/Letter_of_credit_1.jpg" width="500">
</p>

Simple meaning:

> The importer asks the bank to promise the exporter: "If you submit the required complying documents, the bank will make payment according to the LC terms."

## Why is an Import LC Used?

The exporter may not trust the importer enough to sell on open-account terms.

The exporter therefore asks:

> "Give me a bank-backed payment mechanism."

The importer asks its bank to issue an LC.

## Main Parties

```text
Importer
 Applicant
    |
    | Request LC
    v
Issuing Bank
    |
    | LC
    v
Advising Bank
    |
    v
Exporter
 Beneficiary
```

If confirmation is involved:

```text
Issuing Bank ---> Confirming Bank ---> Exporter
```

## Typical Import LC Lifecycle

```text
1. Sales contract
       ↓
2. Importer requests LC
       ↓
3. Issuing bank assesses request
       ↓
4. LC issued
       ↓
5. LC advised to exporter
       ↓
6. Exporter ships goods
       ↓
7. Exporter presents documents
       ↓
8. Banks examine documents
       ↓
9. Complying presentation
       ↓
10. Payment / acceptance / deferred payment
       ↓
11. Importer receives documents
       ↓
12. Import clearance
```

## Common Types of Import LC

### 1. Sight LC

Payment is made when compliant documents are presented and processed according to the LC terms.

**Example:**

Goods value = USD 100,000.

Exporter presents complying documents.

Bank processes payment at sight.

---

### 2. Usance / Deferred Payment LC

Payment occurs after a specified period.

Example:

```text
LC amount = USD 100,000
Tenor = 90 days
```

Exporter presents complying documents.

Payment becomes due according to the 90-day terms.

---

### 3. Confirmed LC

A second bank adds its own undertaking to the LC, subject to the terms of the confirmation.

This can reduce certain bank/country risks perceived by the exporter.

---

### 4. Revolving LC

The LC can automatically restore its available amount under defined conditions.

Useful for recurring shipments.

Example:

```text
Monthly shipment = USD 50,000

Month 1 → 50,000
Month 2 → 50,000
Month 3 → 50,000
...
```

The exact revolving mechanism is defined in the LC.

---

### 5. Transferable LC

An LC that expressly allows transfer to another beneficiary under applicable rules and LC terms.

Useful where an intermediary/trader is involved.

---

### 6. Back-to-Back LC

A second LC is issued based on the security/credit structure of an existing LC.

Often associated with intermediary trade structures.

---

## Import LC Scenario

### Scenario

ABC India purchases machinery from XYZ Germany.

Contract:

```text
Goods value       : EUR 200,000
Payment           : LC
Shipment          : By sea
Required docs     : Invoice, transport document,
                    insurance document, packing list
```

ABC asks its bank to issue an LC.

The German exporter ships the machinery and submits the required documents.

The bank examines the documents against the LC terms.

If the presentation complies with the LC:

```text
Exporter
   ↓
Documents
   ↓
Bank examination
   ↓
Complying presentation
   ↓
Payment according to LC
```

# 7. Import Collection

## Definition

An **Import Collection** is a transaction where documents relating to an import shipment are handled through banks, with payment or acceptance being obtained from the importer according to the collection instructions.

Important distinction:

> In a collection, the bank generally acts as an intermediary for document/payment handling rather than giving the same independent payment undertaking that an LC provides.

## Main Parties

```text
Exporter
   |
   | Documents + collection instruction
   v
Remitting Bank
   |
   v
Collecting / Presenting Bank
   |
   v
Importer
```

## Main Types

### 1. Documents Against Payment (D/P)

The importer gets access to the documents after making payment according to the collection terms.

```text
Documents
   ↓
Importer pays
   ↓
Documents released
```

### 2. Documents Against Acceptance (D/A)

The importer accepts a bill of exchange or other payment undertaking according to the collection terms.

```text
Documents
   ↓
Importer accepts
   ↓
Documents released
   ↓
Payment at maturity
```

## Import Collection Example

Indian company imports goods from Japan.

Exporter does not request an LC but wants control over shipping documents.

The Japanese exporter sends the documents through its bank.

The documents reach the Indian collecting bank.

### D/P example

```text
Indian importer
      |
      | Pays
      v
Indian bank
      |
      | Releases documents
      v
Importer
```

The importer can use the documents to complete the applicable import/clearance process.

---

# 8. Import LC vs Import Collection

| Feature | Import LC | Import Collection |
|---|---|---|
| Bank undertaking | LC creates a bank undertaking subject to its terms | Collection is primarily document/payment handling |
| Risk to exporter | Generally lower than collection/open account, subject to LC terms and bank/country risk | Generally higher because payment depends on buyer/payment arrangements |
| Document examination | Documents examined against LC terms | Documents handled according to collection instructions/rules |
| Cost | Usually higher | Usually lower |
| Common terms | Sight/deferred | D/P or D/A |
| Typical use | Higher-risk or bank-supported trade | Established relationships / lower-cost structure |

# 9. Export Letter of Credit (Export LC)

## Definition

An **Export Letter of Credit** is an LC received by the exporter's bank for an exporter/beneficiary.

From the exporter's point of view, an importer's bank has issued an LC in favor of the exporter.

Simple meaning:

> **Import LC from the importer's point of view = Export LC from the exporter's point of view.**

## Flow

```text
Importer
   |
   | Requests LC
   v
Issuing Bank
   |
   | LC
   v
Exporter Bank
   |
   v
Exporter
```

## Export LC Lifecycle

```text
1. Export sales contract
       ↓
2. Importer requests LC
       ↓
3. Issuing bank issues LC
       ↓
4. Exporter's bank receives/advises LC
       ↓
5. Exporter checks LC
       ↓
6. Exporter ships goods
       ↓
7. Exporter prepares documents
       ↓
8. Documents presented
       ↓
9. Documents examined
       ↓
10. Complying presentation
       ↓
11. Payment / acceptance / deferred payment
```

## Important Exporter Checks

The exporter should examine:

- Applicant name
- Beneficiary name
- LC amount
- Currency
- Expiry date
- Latest shipment date
- Port/place of shipment
- Port/place of destination
- Required documents
- Insurance requirement
- Partial shipment conditions
- Transshipment conditions
- Payment terms
- Presentation period
- Applicable rules
- Any special conditions

## Export LC Scenario

ABC India exports textile products to a US buyer.

The buyer arranges an LC through a US bank.

The LC is advised to ABC's bank.

ABC checks the LC.

ABC ships the goods and submits the required documents.

If the documents comply with the LC:

```text
Complying documents
        ↓
Bank processing
        ↓
Payment according to LC terms
        ↓
Exporter receives proceeds
```

# 10. Export Collection

## Definition

An **Export Collection** is a transaction where the exporter sends commercial/shipping documents through its bank to a bank in the importer's country for collection of payment or acceptance.

## Flow

```text
Exporter
   |
   | Documents + collection instruction
   v
Exporter's Bank
   |
   v
Importer's Bank
   |
   v
Importer
```

## Types

### 1. D/P — Documents Against Payment

Importer pays before receiving documents according to the collection instructions.

```text
Documents → Bank
              ↓
           Importer pays
              ↓
        Documents released
```

### 2. D/A — Documents Against Acceptance

Importer accepts the payment obligation.

Documents are released according to the collection terms.

Payment occurs at maturity.

## Export Collection Example

Indian exporter sells goods to a buyer in Singapore.

They agree:

> Documents Against Payment.

Exporter ships the goods.

Documents go through the banking channel.

The Singapore buyer pays.

The collecting bank releases documents according to the collection instructions.

---

# 11. Export LC vs Export Collection

| Feature | Export LC | Export Collection |
|---|---|---|
| Payment structure | Bank undertaking under LC terms | Collection of payment/acceptance |
| Bank's role | LC undertaking/advising/confirmation/nominated role as applicable | Collection/document handling |
| Exporter's protection | Depends on LC terms, complying presentation, and bank/country risk | More dependent on buyer's willingness/ability to pay |
| Cost | Usually higher | Usually lower |
| Common use | Higher payment assurance requirement | Established buyer relationships |

# 12. Shipping Guarantee

## Definition

A **Shipping Guarantee** is a bank-issued undertaking that may allow an importer to obtain delivery of goods from a carrier when the original transport document is not yet available, subject to the bank's terms and applicable law/practice.

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/naveen-53/Learning-Pathways/main/Images/Letter_of_credit_1.jpg" width="500">
</p>


It is often relevant when:

- Goods have arrived
- Original transport document is delayed
- Importer needs to take delivery
- Bank is willing to provide the required guarantee/undertaking

## Problem Scenario

Imagine:

```text
Ship arrives at port
        ↓
Goods available
        ↓
Original Bill of Lading not yet received
        ↓
Importer cannot easily obtain delivery
```

If the importer waits:

- Storage charges may increase
- Demurrage may occur
- Production may be delayed

A shipping guarantee can be used in eligible circumstances.

## Example

Indian importer purchases machinery from Germany.

The vessel reaches Chennai port.

But the original Bill of Lading is delayed.

The importer asks its bank for a Shipping Guarantee.

Subject to bank approval and applicable requirements:

```text
Importer
   ↓
Request Shipping Guarantee
   ↓
Bank evaluates
   ↓
Bank issues guarantee
   ↓
Carrier / shipping line
   ↓
Goods released according to applicable process
```

## Important Risk

The bank may undertake an obligation toward the carrier.

Therefore the bank normally requires appropriate security/credit approval and documentation.

## Simple Memory

```text
Goods arrived
+
Original transport document delayed
=
Shipping Guarantee may solve delivery problem
```

# 13. Bank Guarantee (BG)

## Definition

A **Bank Guarantee** is an undertaking issued by a bank to a beneficiary, on behalf of its customer/applicant, under which the bank agrees to pay the beneficiary if the specified conditions for invocation of the guarantee are met.

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://github.com/naveen-53/Learning-Pathways/blob/main/Images/Bank_gurantee.jpg" width="500">
</p>


Simple meaning:

> The bank provides a financial undertaking supporting the customer's obligation.

## Parties

```text
Applicant
(Customer)
    |
    | Requests BG
    v
Issuing Bank
    |
    | Guarantee
    v
Beneficiary
```

## Common Types of Bank Guarantee

### 1. Performance Guarantee

Protects the beneficiary if the applicant fails to perform contractual obligations, subject to guarantee terms.

**Example:**

A contractor receives a construction contract.

The customer asks for a performance guarantee.

---

### 2. Financial Guarantee

Supports a financial obligation.

**Example:**

A company must make a specified payment under a contract.

The beneficiary requires a bank guarantee.

---

### 3. Advance Payment Guarantee

Used where a buyer pays an advance to a seller/contractor.

The guarantee protects the beneficiary according to its terms if the advance is not properly accounted for.

---

### 4. Bid Bond / Tender Guarantee

Supports a bidder's obligations during a tender process.

---

### 5. Retention Money Guarantee

May be used instead of retaining a portion of contract payments, subject to contract terms.

---

### 6. Customs / Statutory Guarantee

Used in eligible situations where a government/customs authority requires a bank undertaking.

---

### 7. Shipping Guarantee

A specialized guarantee/undertaking connected with delivery of goods when original shipping documents are unavailable, subject to applicable requirements.

---

# 14. Bank Guarantee Scenario

## Performance Guarantee Example

ABC Construction receives a project worth ₹10 crore.

The customer says:

> "Provide a performance guarantee of ₹1 crore."

ABC approaches its bank.

The bank evaluates the customer and issues the guarantee.

If ABC performs the contract:

```text
Project completed
      ↓
No invocation
      ↓
Guarantee expires/released according to terms
```

If the beneficiary makes a valid invocation according to the guarantee:

```text
Beneficiary
     ↓
Invocation
     ↓
Bank checks applicable terms
     ↓
Payment if conditions are met
```

The bank may then have a claim against its customer under the underlying banking arrangement.

---

# 15. Bank Guarantee vs Letter of Credit

| Feature | Bank Guarantee | Letter of Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Support an obligation/default risk | Facilitate payment against complying LC documents |
| Typical trigger | Valid invocation according to guarantee | Complying presentation under LC |
| Common parties | Applicant, issuing bank, beneficiary | Applicant, issuing bank, beneficiary, advising/nominated/confirming banks as applicable |
| Example | Performance guarantee | Import purchase |
| Primary concept | "If specified obligation/default condition occurs, bank pays according to guarantee" | "If documents comply with LC terms, bank pays/undertakes payment according to LC" |

# 16. Finance Request

## Definition

A **Finance Request** in Trade Finance generally means a customer request for bank financing associated with an import, export, or trade transaction.

Simple meaning:

> The customer has a trade transaction but needs funding from the bank.

## Why Finance is Needed

Trade creates a timing gap.

Example:

```text
Today:
Exporter ships goods

But:

Importer receives goods later
and
Customer's cash may be tied up
```

Or:

```text
Customer must pay supplier today
but
will receive money from its buyer after 60 days
```

The bank may provide eligible trade finance to bridge the timing gap.

## Common Finance Categories

Exact product names differ between banks, but trade finance can include:

### Import Finance

Finance provided to support eligible import payment/obligations.

### Export Finance

Finance provided to support eligible export transactions.

### Pre-shipment Finance

Funding provided before shipment to support production/procurement of export goods.

### Post-shipment Finance

Funding provided after shipment while export proceeds are awaited.

### Bill / Document Finance

Financing against eligible trade documents or receivables.

### Loan Against Import Documents

Eligible financing associated with import documents.

## Finance Request Example

ABC India imports machinery worth ₹50 lakh.

Supplier requires payment.

ABC does not want to use all its working capital immediately.

ABC submits a finance request.

Bank evaluates:

- Customer eligibility
- Facility limit
- Transaction details
- Documents
- Security
- Tenor
- Pricing
- Repayment source
- Compliance

If approved:

```text
Import transaction
       ↓
Finance request
       ↓
Credit assessment
       ↓
Approval
       ↓
Disbursement / settlement
       ↓
Customer repays
```

# 17. Import Regularization

## Definition

**Import regularization** is the process of completing and reconciling the required regulatory, banking, and documentary requirements for an import transaction after the relevant import payment/transaction has occurred.

Simple meaning:

> The bank/customer ensures that an import transaction is properly completed, documented, matched, and reported according to applicable requirements.

## Why Regularization is Required

An import transaction can involve multiple events:

```text
Import order
    ↓
Payment
    ↓
Shipment
    ↓
Customs clearance
    ↓
Documents
    ↓
Regulatory reporting
    ↓
Transaction closure/reconciliation
```

The bank needs to ensure the transaction does not remain unresolved because of missing documents or mismatched information.

## Example

Indian company imports goods worth USD 100,000.

Payment has been made.

But the required import documentation/clearance evidence is not yet matched to the payment.

The transaction remains pending for documentation/regularization.

Later:

```text
Import payment
      +
Import documentation
      +
Customs/transaction evidence
      ↓
Matching / verification
      ↓
Regularization / closure
```

## Common Reasons a Transaction May Need Follow-up

- Import documents not received
- Payment amount differs from shipment value
- Shipment delayed
- Goods not received
- Documents have discrepancies
- Partial shipment
- Refund received
- Cancellation
- Overpayment/underpayment
- Data mismatch
- Regulatory reporting issue

## Scenario

### Case 1 — Normal import

```text
Payment = USD 50,000
Import value = USD 50,000
Documents = Available
```

Transaction can be reconciled normally.

### Case 2 — Mismatch

```text
Payment = USD 50,000
Import document = USD 48,000
```

The bank/customer needs to investigate the USD 2,000 difference and determine the appropriate treatment.

# 18. Export Regularization

## Definition

**Export regularization** is the process of completing and reconciling the required banking, regulatory, and documentary requirements for an export transaction, including ensuring that export proceeds and supporting documents are appropriately accounted for.

Simple meaning:

> The exporter has shipped goods/services, and the bank must ensure the export transaction and its proceeds are properly completed and reconciled.

## Export Lifecycle

```text
Export order
     ↓
Production / procurement
     ↓
Shipment
     ↓
Shipping documents
     ↓
Export reporting / documentation
     ↓
Foreign buyer payment
     ↓
Inward remittance
     ↓
Matching / reconciliation
     ↓
Regularization / closure
```

## Example

ABC India exports goods worth USD 75,000.

The goods are shipped.

The buyer later sends USD 75,000.

The bank receives the inward remittance and matches it with the export transaction.

```text
Export transaction
       +
Shipping evidence
       +
USD 75,000 inward remittance
       ↓
Matching
       ↓
Regularization / closure
```

## Cases Requiring Follow-up

### Case 1 — Full payment

```text
Export value = USD 100,000
Received      = USD 100,000
```

Straightforward reconciliation, subject to applicable requirements.

### Case 2 — Partial payment

```text
Export value = USD 100,000
Received      = USD 70,000
```

Remaining USD 30,000 requires follow-up according to applicable rules and circumstances.

### Case 3 — Excess payment

```text
Export value = USD 100,000
Received      = USD 105,000
```

The additional USD 5,000 must be explained and treated appropriately.

### Case 4 — Goods returned/cancelled

The export transaction may require additional documentation and regulatory handling.

---

# 19. Import Regularization vs Export Regularization

| Feature | Import Regularization | Export Regularization |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction direction | Goods/services coming into country | Goods/services going out |
| Main concern | Import payment + import documentation | Export shipment + export proceeds |
| Typical money movement | Outward payment | Inward receipt |
| Reconciliation | Payment vs import evidence | Export transaction vs inward proceeds |
| Example | USD 100k paid for machinery | USD 100k received for exported machinery |

# 28. Important Difference: LC vs Collection

This is one of the most important concepts in Trade.

## Letter of Credit

Think:

> **Bank undertaking + documents complying with LC terms**

```text
LC
 |
 +-- Issuing bank undertaking
 |
 +-- Specific terms
 |
 +-- Document presentation
 |
 +-- Examination
 |
 +-- Payment/undertaking if compliant
```

## Collection

Think:

> **Bank-mediated document and payment/acceptance process**

```text
Collection
 |
 +-- Exporter sends documents
 |
 +-- Banks forward/present documents
 |
 +-- Importer pays or accepts
 |
 +-- Documents released according to instructions
```

---

# 29. D/P vs D/A

| D/P | D/A |
|---|---|
| Documents Against Payment | Documents Against Acceptance |
| Payment first | Acceptance first |
| Documents released against payment according to terms | Documents released against acceptance according to terms |
| Buyer generally pays before obtaining documents | Buyer pays at maturity |
| Lower payment-time exposure for seller than D/A, but collection risk remains | Greater credit/time exposure to seller |

Memory trick:

```text
D/P = Pay → Documents

D/A = Accept → Documents → Pay Later
```

---

# 30. Sight vs Usance

## Sight

```text
Complying presentation
       ↓
Payment according to LC terms
```

## Usance / Deferred

```text
Complying presentation
       ↓
Payment undertaking / acceptance
       ↓
Maturity date
       ↓
Payment
```

Memory trick:

```text
SIGHT  = NOW / at sight
USANCE = LATER
```

# 32. Document Discrepancy

A **discrepancy** occurs when presented documents do not comply with the applicable LC terms or other required documentary conditions.

Examples:

- Shipment after permitted date
- Incorrect beneficiary name
- Amount mismatch
- Missing document
- Incorrect port
- Inconsistent goods description
- Transport document issue
- Insurance requirement not met

## Example

LC says:

```text
Latest shipment date = 10 September
```

Transport document indicates:

```text
Shipment date = 15 September
```

This can create a documentary discrepancy under the LC terms.

# 35. Important Business Terms

## Applicant

The customer requesting the bank to issue an instrument.

## Beneficiary

The party in whose favor the instrument is issued.

## Issuing Bank

Bank issuing the LC or guarantee.

## Advising Bank

Bank that advises an LC to the beneficiary.

## Confirmation

An additional bank undertaking added to an LC, subject to the terms of the confirmation.

## Expiry Date

The date after which an LC/guarantee is no longer available or effective according to its terms.

## Shipment Date

Date by which shipment must occur according to an LC.

## Presentation

Submission of documents to the relevant bank under an LC.

## Discrepancy

A deviation from required documentary conditions.

## Maturity Date

Date when a deferred payment/accepted obligation becomes due.

## Tenor

The period from the relevant start date to maturity.

## Settlement

Completion of the financial obligation.

## Regularization

Completion/reconciliation of the required transaction records and supporting evidence.

# 36. Scenario-Based Learning

## Scenario 1 — Customer imports goods and needs immediate payment

**Problem:** Importer needs to pay a foreign supplier.

Possible products:

- Import LC
- Import Collection
- Outward Remittance
- Import Finance

---

## Scenario 2 — Exporter wants bank-supported payment structure

**Problem:** Exporter does not want to rely only on the importer's promise to pay.

Possible product:

- Export LC

---

## Scenario 3 — Exporter trusts buyer and wants a simpler process

**Problem:** Established buyer relationship.

Possible product:

- Export Collection

Possible terms:

- D/P
- D/A

---

## Scenario 4 — Importer needs goods but original transport document is delayed

**Problem:** Vessel has arrived but original document is unavailable.

Possible product:

- Shipping Guarantee

---

## Scenario 5 — Contractor needs to provide contractual security

**Problem:** Beneficiary asks for assurance that contractor will meet its obligations.

Possible product:

- Performance Bank Guarantee

---

## Scenario 6 — Importer needs working capital

**Problem:** Import transaction exists but customer needs financing.

Possible product:

- Trade Finance / Finance Request

---

## Scenario 7 — Export proceeds have not been matched

**Problem:**

```text
Export transaction = USD 100,000
Received = USD 80,000
```

Possible action:

- Investigate outstanding USD 20,000
- Check transaction/document details
- Perform applicable follow-up and regularization

---

## Scenario 8 — Import payment and documentation mismatch

**Problem:**

```text
Payment = USD 100,000
Import documentation = USD 95,000
```

Possible action:

- Investigate difference
- Check amendments/refunds/charges/partial shipments
- Complete applicable regulatory/documentary reconciliation

# 38. One-Line Definitions for Interview Revision

| Topic | One-line definition |
|---|---|
| Inward Remittance | Receipt of money from an overseas source into the country |
| Outward Remittance | Sending money from the country to an overseas beneficiary |
| Import LC | Bank-issued undertaking supporting an importer's payment against complying documents |
| Import Collection | Bank-mediated collection of import documents/payment or acceptance |
| Export LC | LC received/advised for an exporter in favor of the exporter |
| Export Collection | Exporter's documents sent through banks for collection of payment/acceptance |
| Shipping Guarantee | Bank undertaking that may facilitate delivery when original transport documents are unavailable, subject to applicable terms |
| Bank Guarantee | Bank undertaking to pay a beneficiary if the specified conditions for invocation are met |
| Finance Request | Customer request for bank financing connected with a trade transaction |
| Import Regularization | Completion and reconciliation of import transaction documentation/payment and applicable regulatory requirements |
| Export Regularization | Completion and reconciliation of export transaction/shipment/proceeds and applicable regulatory requirements |
